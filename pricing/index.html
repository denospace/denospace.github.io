Pricing

Denospace is two things: an editor for your Org library, and a publisher that turns part of it into a website. They are priced separately, because they are useful separately.

Not open yet. Hosting is not available for sign-up. Self-hosting works today and always will. These prices are what hosting will cost when it opens.

Self-hosted — free, forever

MIT licensed. Run it on your own machine, against your own folder, with every feature. No account, no limits, no telemetry. See Self-hosting.

This is not a crippled tier. It is the same software.

Hosted — $8 / month

One library, hosted and maintained: indexed, upgraded, backed up, reachable from a phone, with TLS and a server you don't run. Your notes stay in your own storage — see Dropbox.

There is no free hosted tier. Hosting other people's files costs real money continuously, and a free tier would be funded by the people paying for it. If you want free, self-host: that genuinely is free, and it is the same product.

Publishing — $6 / month per site

Render notes to a website and publish them. Priced per site, independent of hosting — you can publish from a self-hosted instance. See Publishing.

Both — $12 / month

Hosting plus one published site.

What the subscription pays for

Worth being direct about this, since the software is free to self-host and you are entitled to ask what the money is actually for.

It funds the one thing on the roadmap that a side project cannot reach: a native iOS client with a native text editor.

The web app is built on CodeMirror, which is genuinely excellent — on a desktop. On a phone, a browser-based editor is always slightly wrong. Text selection fights the system, the keyboard misbehaves, scrolling is approximate, offline is fragile, and the battery notices. The gap between adequate and good on a phone is a native text view, and there is no shortcut to one.

That is a large piece of work — a second codebase, a release process, and months rather than weekends — which is exactly why it needs revenue instead of evenings. No date is promised. But it is the thing the subscription is for, and it will not happen without one.

It does not need to out-feature the dedicated Org task apps, several of which are good. It needs to carry your whole library with its links intact, handle SCHEDULED and DEADLINE, and show a usable agenda. Most of the rest is already built.

Storage

Not sold by the gigabyte, because we don't hold your notes — your own storage does, and you already pay for that. There is a generous fair-use limit rather than a tier, and you will not be billed for writing a lot.
